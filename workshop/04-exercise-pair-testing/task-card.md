# Exercise 4: Pair Testing + Improve
**Time:** 20 minutes  
**Goal:** Get feedback on your prototype (or direction) from another person — then improve it

---

## What You'll Do

Test with a partner. One person tests, one observes. Switch. Then change one thing based on what you saw.

**This is lightweight** — not formal usability testing. But the loop is the real thing: watch someone use it → find where it breaks → fix that.

---

## Setup (2 min)

1. **Check Slack** for your pair assignment (posted before the session)
2. **Mixed pairs:** remote person screen-shares (or shares the HTML file so the tester drives)
3. **Brief your partner on their role** — the maker from your hypothesis: *"You're a designer who's never opened Devboxes."* Nothing more — don't explain the UI.
4. **The task is your success sentence** from Define, phrased as a goal. Use `prompt-template.md` if you want the AI to phrase it.

---

## Round 1 (4 min) → Round 2 (4 min)

### For the person showing

1. Give **one task** — your success sentence as a goal
2. **Don't explain. Don't help. Don't redesign mid-test.**
3. Before each click, ask: *"What do you expect to happen?"*
4. Note **where they hesitate**, what they say out loud, and where they go off path
5. At the end ask: *"What was confusing or unclear?"*

### For the person testing

1. Play the role you were given
2. Try to complete the task
3. Think out loud
4. Be honest — polite but direct

---

## Improve (7 min)

1. Pick **the one moment they hesitated most**
2. Change only that — copy, order, a missing state, a label. Ask the AI: *"My tester hesitated at [X] because [Y]. Change only that."*
3. No prototype? Rewrite your direction in one sentence to fix it.

---

## Example tasks

- "You're a designer. Open this app and try to start a new flow without asking anyone for help."
- "You need a working environment for a new service. Get as far as you can without Slack."
- "Find out whether your change is live in production."

---

## Output

Post in the Slack thread, one line each:
- **Surprised me:** …
- **I changed:** …

Be ready to share it in the close.

---

## A note on who tested

Your partner is an engineer playing a role — useful practice, but not your user. The real test is showing it to **one actual maker of that role this week**.
